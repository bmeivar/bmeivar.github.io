Research

I study when and why visual representations break: how object retrieval degrades with scale, viewpoint and context, how to evaluate retrieval at web scale at a fraction of the cost, and whether vision-language models actually use the evidence in front of them.

I design controlled experiments that vary one factor at a time (object scale, camera distance, visible context, gallery size) and measure its effect across many pretrained backbones and open VLMs from 3B to 397B parameters. The results lead to methods, such as scale-robust retrieval that sets the state of the art on ILIAS-100M, and to cheaper evaluation protocols. Before computer vision I worked in aeroelasticity, where I built a data-driven flutter warning system that was validated in flight tests.

Scale and context in retrieval

Object-to-image ratio mismatch, not resolution, drives most cross-scale failures. Fixing it on the query side reaches 42.0 mAP@1000 on ILIAS-100M without re-indexing the gallery.

Retrieval evaluation at scale

A per-query scaling law recovers 100M-image mAP to within about 0.01 from a 5% sample, with 20× less indexing.

Evidence use in VLMs

Ill-posed tasks such as metric size estimation expose which visual cues a model relies on, where well-posed tasks hide it.

  2. arXivOct 2026

    Beyond Resolution: Object-to-Image Ratio Mismatch in Instance Retrieval

    Boaz Meivar, Ofir Kedem, Amit Edenzon, Gal Chechik, Shai Avidan

    On 3,021 Objaverse objects rendered at five camera distances, over 80% of the cross-distance retrieval loss comes from object-to-image ratio mismatch rather than resolution, for 9 of 12 pretrained backbones. Query-side scale augmentation plus an OWLv2 crop reranker reaches state of the art on ILIAS-100M (42.0 mAP@1000) without retraining or modifying the gallery index, and a LoRA fine-tune matches the query-side gains in a single forward pass.

  3. arXivOct 2026

    Web Scale on a Budget: Tail-Aware Retrieval Evaluation

    Boaz Meivar, Shai Avidan

    Retrieval evaluation obeys a scaling law: the number of distractors that outrank a positive is binomial in gallery size. From a 5% sample (20× less indexing), the tail-aware estimate matches full 100M-scale mAP to within about 0.01 across five backbones and three benchmarks (ILIAS, INSTRE, GLDv2), an order of magnitude better than scoring the subset directly.
