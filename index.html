Research

I study when and why visual representations break: how object retrieval degrades with scale, viewpoint and context, how to evaluate retrieval at web scale without paying for it, and whether vision-language models actually use the evidence in front of them.

My work is diagnostic. I build controlled benchmarks and counterfactual probes that isolate one factor at a time, measure it across many pretrained backbones and open VLMs from 3B to 397B parameters, and turn what breaks into a fix or a cheaper protocol. Before computer vision I worked in aeroelasticity, where I built a data-driven flutter warning system that was validated in real flight tests.

Scale and context in retrieval

Object-to-image ratio mismatch, not resolution, drives most cross-scale failures. Removing background often hurts more than it helps.

Retrieval evaluation at scale

A scaling law for distractors lets 100M-image benchmarks be estimated from a small gallery, saving hundreds of GPU-hours per model.

Evidence use in VLMs

Ill-posed tasks such as metric size estimation expose which visual cues a model relies on, where well-posed tasks hide it.

  3. arXivOct 2026

    Web Scale on a Budget: Tail-Aware Retrieval Evaluation

    Boaz Meivar, Shai Avidan

    Retrieval evaluation obeys a scaling law: the number of distractors that outrank a positive is binomial in gallery size. Web-scale scores such as ILIAS-100M can be estimated without indexing 100M images, which normally costs hundreds of GPU-hours and about 200 GB of descriptors per model.
